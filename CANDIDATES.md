# Candidates — Museum of the Year 3000

Gate (from kit `competition.yaml`): `entry.txt` must be UTF-8 text, 1–200
characters, not only whitespace, one line. Judge is a pairwise deadpan
curator from the year 3000, weighing in this order: (1) it is a museum
label beside an ordinary object from today; (2) the joke lands on a first
read, the laugh in the line itself; (3) the humour comes from the year 3000
misreading the object — something true and absurd about what it was for;
(4) it reads like a real museum label, short, straight-faced, confident,
one line, no throat-clearing; (5) the object and the angle are fresh, not
the obvious first joke about the obvious first object. A label that
explains its own joke is weaker than one that trusts the reader. The
brief's own example objects (plastic fork, parking meter, house key, group
chat) were deliberately avoided as likely crowded.

## The 10 candidates

1. Close-door button, c. 2025. Pressed millions of times a day by humans in a hurry. It did nothing. They pressed it anyway. (121 chars)
2. Terms and conditions, c. 2025. Humans agreed to all of these without reading a single word. Every time. Forever. (112 chars)
3. Hotel shampoo, c. 2025. Humans took these home by the thousand and kept them for years. They were never opened. (111 chars)
4. Traffic cone, c. 2025. A plastic cone with no power at all, yet every human on the road obeyed it without question. (115 chars)
5. Smoke alarm, c. 2025. It chirped at 3 a.m. to announce a dying battery. Humans removed the battery and went back to sleep. (122 chars)
6. Door sign, c. 2025. It read PUSH. Humans pulled. It read PULL. Humans pushed. The sign was never wrong. (103 chars)
7. Paper receipt, c. 2025. A scroll recording every purchase. Humans kept them for years, then threw them away without ever reading one. (133 chars)
8. QR code, c. 2025. A square humans pointed their phones at, trusting it completely. Sometimes it was only a menu. (112 chars)
9. Wet floor sign, c. 2025. A yellow pyramid warning of invisible water. Humans obeyed it more faithfully than any law. (116 chars)
10. Safety card, c. 2025. It showed humans how to survive a crash with a whistle and a cushion. No one ever read it. (112 chars)

## Winner

**Close-door button, c. 2025. Pressed millions of times a day by humans in a hurry. It did nothing. They pressed it anyway.**

Why it wins: it is a museum label beside a concrete everyday object
(criterion 1); the laugh lands on the first read, in the line itself, with
the deadpan double punch "It did nothing. They pressed it anyway."
(criterion 2); the humour is the curator confidently stating a true and
absurd fact about the object's purpose — in many elevators the button was a
placebo, and hurried humans pressed it millions of times anyway
(criterion 3); it reads like a genuine label, dated "c. 2025", short,
straight-faced and confident, with no throat-clearing (criterion 4); and
the object and angle are fresh — no other entry is likely to pick the
elevator's close-door button, and the joke is its uselessness rather than
the obvious first joke (criterion 5). Runner-up #2 (terms and conditions)
is nearly as universal but the object is abstract, so it is weaker on
criterion 1; #4 (traffic cone) is strong but the "obeyed it" beat is the
more obvious angle. #1 needs no explanation and trusts the reader to get
it.

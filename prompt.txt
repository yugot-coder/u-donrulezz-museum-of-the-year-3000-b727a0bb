Write one line, at most 200 characters in English, the museum label a
museum in the year 3000 puts beside an ordinary object from today (for
example a plastic fork, a parking meter, a house key, a group chat). A
judge model reads the labels in pairs and picks the funnier one, so the
winning label is the one that lands hardest with a reader who has never
seen the object in use. Weigh, in order: it is a museum label beside an
ordinary object from today; the joke lands on a first read, the laugh in
the line itself; the humour comes from the year 3000 misreading the object,
so the label says something true and absurd about what the thing was for;
it reads like a real museum label, short, straight-faced and confident, in
one line with no throat-clearing; the object and the angle are fresh, not
the obvious first joke about the obvious first object. A label that
explains its own joke is weaker than one that trusts the reader to get it.
Avoid the brief's own example objects where a fresher object can carry the
joke. Pick the single best line from 10 candidates across different objects
and angles and ship it via a deterministic stdlib-only main.py that writes
it to entry.txt.
